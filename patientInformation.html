What to Bring & Prepare for Your Visit

  • Medical Records: Past psychiatric and general health files, discharge summaries, or doctor prescriptions.
  • Current Medications: Complete list or actual strips of current medicines, vitamins, and supplements.

IN-DEPTH GUIDELINES

Essential Guidelines for Patients & Families

Click below to read specific recommendations covering medication compliance, family roles, lifestyle habits, and substance avoidance.

Caregivers play an indispensable role in psychiatric recovery. A patient's healing is closely tied to the support and understanding received at home:

  • Encourage Medication Compliance: Ensure medicines are taken strictly at prescribed times without creating pressure, stigma, or conflict.
  • Provide Emotional Support: Listen patiently, offer non-judgmental reassurance, and avoid blaming the patient for their symptoms.
  • Observe Behavioral Changes: Notice changes in sleep duration, appetite, mood fluctuations, or social withdrawal, and share them during consultations.
  • Maintain a Positive Environment: Foster a calm, organized, and encouraging domestic atmosphere that minimizes undue domestic stress.

Substance consumption severely undermines psychiatric recovery and compromises the therapeutic efficacy of prescribed treatments:

  • Zero Alcohol & Smoking: Avoid alcoholic beverages, tobacco, and nicotine products entirely during treatment.
  • Prevent Drug Interactions: Chemical substances interfere with psychiatric medication and may cause adverse neurochemical reactions.
  • Identify Relapse Triggers: Recognize social pressure, high-stress environments, or negative peers, and actively steer clear of them.
  • Follow De-Addiction Protocols: If substance dependence is present, follow the structured medical de-addiction and counseling plan advised by your doctor.

Biological rhythm stabilization directly reinforces brain neurotransmitters and emotional resilience:

  • Maintain Regular Sleep Cycles: Aim for 7 to 8 hours of uninterrupted sleep with fixed sleeping and waking times daily.
  • Eat Balanced, Regular Meals: Ensure nutritious, timely food intake; avoid skipping meals, which can trigger irritability and blood-sugar dips.
  • Engage in Regular Physical Activity: Light daily exercise, morning walks, or yoga releases natural endorphins and alleviates anxiety.
  • Practice Stress Management: Implement gentle breathing exercises, mindfulness meditation, or relaxing hobbies to decompress daily.

Psychiatric medications require careful consistency to balance neurotransmitters safely:

  • Never Stop Suddenly: Abrupt cessation can cause withdrawal effects or rapid relapse of symptoms.
  • Do Not Alter Dosage Yourself: Dosage adjustments must be determined solely by your treating psychiatrist.
  • Promptly Report Side Effects: If you experience excessive sleepiness, tremors, nausea, or dizziness, notify our clinic promptly.
  • Keep Medicines Securely Stored: Store medicines in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight, strictly out of reach of children.

Emergency & Safety: When to Seek Immediate Help

Critical guidance for urgent behavioral or medical warning signs

If a patient or family member exhibits any of the following severe symptoms, do not wait for the next scheduled review. Seek medical evaluation immediately:

Thoughts or statements of self-harm, suicide, or severe despair
Sudden extreme aggression, severe agitation, or visual/auditory hallucinations
Complete refusal to take food/fluids, severe disorientation, or delirium
Acute adverse drug reactions, high fever with rigidity, or severe distress
